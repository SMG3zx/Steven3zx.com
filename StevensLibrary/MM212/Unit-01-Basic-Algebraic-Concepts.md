# MM212 College Algebra: Unit 1, Basic Algebraic Concepts

**Course:** MM212 College Algebra (Purdue Global, 5 credit hours)
**Text:** College Algebra Essentials, 6th ed., Robert F. Blitzer (Pearson)
**Course outcome practiced:** MM212-1, Manipulate problems involving polynomials.

## Unit overview

Unit 1 covers the foundation everything else in the course is built on: types of numbers, signed-number rules, exponents, and the order of operations. The unit ends with evaluating polynomials for given values. The course presents the order of operations as a routine you will eventually perform without thinking, like the order of your morning routine. Practice is what makes it automatic.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Simplify expressions using the order of operations.
- Simplify algebraic expressions.
- Evaluate algebraic expressions.
- Simplify exponential expressions.
- Evaluate exponential expressions.
- Identify polynomials and their degrees.

## Key concepts

### Types of numbers and signed numbers
- Know the number families (natural, whole, integer, rational, real) and where each sits on the number line.
- **Subtraction is addition of the opposite.** The additive inverse of a number is its opposite, so 3 − 1 = 3 + (−1). Use this to avoid sign errors when simplifying.

### Exponents
- An exponent counts repeated multiplication: aⁿ means a multiplied by itself n times.
- Simplify exponential expressions using the exponent rules before combining terms.

### Order of operations
1. Parentheses and other grouping symbols
2. Exponents
3. Multiplication and division, left to right
4. Addition and subtraction, left to right

### Algebraic and polynomial expressions
- A **variable** is a letter that stands for an unknown amount. An **expression** combines numbers and variables without an equals sign.
- To **evaluate** an expression, substitute the given value for the variable, then apply the order of operations.
- A **polynomial** is a sum of terms whose variables have whole-number exponents. The **degree** of a term is the sum of its variable exponents. The **degree of a polynomial** is the highest degree among its terms.

## Connection to the unit's discussion: mathematical modeling

Algebra describes the world by using variables to represent unknown amounts. The course calls this a **mathematical model**. Unit 1's discussion asks you to build one for a home budget:

- Define variables for at least two Needs, two Wants, and two Savings items.
- Write inequalities for the 50/30/20 rule, using "less than or equal to":
  - Need1 + Need2 ≤ 50% (Monthly Budget)
  - Want1 + Want2 ≤ 30% (Monthly Budget)
  - Savings1 + Savings2 ≤ 20% (Monthly Budget)
- Share no actual currency amounts. Use variables and percentages only.

The 50/30/20 rule is very generalized. It is a starting point, not a fixed law.

## Discussion requirements

- Post 1: Initial model, as above.
- Post 2: Reply to a classmate on alternative budgeting approaches (changed percentages or added categories). Optional: write an equation for the alternative.
- Post 3: Reply to a second classmate on reviewing and adjusting a budget model, plus any habits or tricks that help.
- Every post: on topic, original, at least 100 words, with frequent references to unit material and citations to the textbook and online sources.
